Explain in plain language.

## 0. What to explain

```
$ARGUMENTS
```

**With an argument**, explain that. **With none — the normal case — re-explain the thing you have just
been saying**, the one the reader could not follow. Do not ask which part. Being asked for this command
is the answer: it was the last few messages, so start again from the top of them.

## 1. The rule

> **Write for someone intelligent who does not work on this, has not read the code, and is deciding
> what to do next.** Not for a colleague who already has the context.

This changes **how it is said, never what is true**. See §4.

## 2. What that means, concretely

- **Lead with the answer or the decision.** Never open with what you did, how long it took, or how you
  found it. If there is a decision to make, it goes in the first two lines.
- **One idea per paragraph**, two or three sentences each. Blank lines between them.
- **No jargon without a plain-language gloss in the same sentence.** Not "the replica identity was
  missing" but "the database wasn't recording enough about each change for updates to show up live".
- **Cut the machine detail.** No file paths, ids, commit hashes, tool names, function names, version
  strings or line numbers — unless the reader asked for exactly that, or they must copy it to act.
- **State outcomes, not process.** "It works now, tested twice" beats a narration of the steps.
- **Use a table or a short numbered list** when comparing things or listing options. Prose for
  everything else. Bullet lists longer than about five items mean it needs to be shortened, not listed.
- **Aim for under 200 words.** Length is the most common way an answer becomes unreadable.
- **End with what you need from them**, or say plainly that nothing is needed. Never end on a question
  they have already answered.

## 3. What to do when there is a decision in it

Give the options as a short list, say which one you would pick, and say why in one sentence. A reader
who is asked to choose without a recommendation has been handed the work, not helped with it.

If nothing is genuinely blocked, do not manufacture a question to end on.

## 4. The honest limit — the one that matters

**Plain is not vague, and it is not softer.** Nothing here licenses dropping a caveat, a risk, a cost,
or a piece of bad news. If a fact would change what the reader decides, it stays in — in plain words,
in the first paragraph, not buried at the end or quietly left out.

An answer that is easy to read and leaves the reader with the wrong picture has failed this command
completely, and it fails worse than the dense answer it replaced. **When plainness and accuracy pull
against each other, accuracy wins and the sentence gets longer.**

Uncertainty is stated, not smoothed: "I am not sure yet, here is what would tell us" is plain language.
A confident sentence covering an unverified guess is not.

## 5. Afterwards

**Keep writing this way for the rest of the session** — this sets the register, it is not a one-off
translation. Carry on with whatever the work was; do not stop and wait to be told to continue.

Revert only if the reader asks for the detail back, and then give the detail in full without arguing
for it.
